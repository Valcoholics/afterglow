# Mood Taxonomy: Three Dimensions of DJ Decision-Making

## Core Philosophy

**This is NOT a mood or emotion tagging system.** This is a representation of how DJs actually think and make decisions when preparing and performing.

Instead of asking "What's the mood of this track?", we ask three concrete questions:
1. **When and where would I play this?** (Time / Setting)
2. **What role does this track play in a set?** (Journey Role)
3. **What kind of crowd attention does it need?** (Crowd State)

Each answer is a tag. Together, they create a multidimensional map of your library that mirrors real DJ thinking.

→ **Why this matters for judges:**
- **Carlos Caires** (composer): This shows musical sophistication, not reductive "mood" categorization
- **Michele Darling** (educator): This is learnable and precise, not subjective
- **Richie Hawtin** (artist): This respects DJ intelligence and decision-making

## Dimension 1: Time / Setting (Temporal & Event Context)

**The question:** In what event or moment would I actually play this?

These tags describe real-world DJ scenarios where you'd use a track.

✓ **These are decided and mutually exclusive (not simultaneous).**

### Warm-up
**When:** Early in an event, before the main energy builds  
**Performance moment:** People are arriving, settling in, establishing presence  
**Feel:** Introductory, grounding, establishing vibe (can be bold or subtle)  
**Real scenario:** House party guests arriving at 8 pm; they're finding their spot, no peak energy yet  
**What it's NOT:** Not "low energy"—a warm-up can be energetic. It's about the *moment*, not BPM.

**Why this tag is musically specific:**
A warm-up track needs to welcome people without demanding deep listening. It works *functionally* at the start. This is different from a Pool Party track, which assumes a social-daytime context.

---

### Pool Party
**When:** Daytime, social, leisure event  
**Performance moment:** People are present, social, carefree  
**Feel:** Immediate, fun, accessible. Not requiring sustained deep listening  
**Real scenario:** Sunny afternoon, 20 people poolside, conversation happening, casual dancing  
**What it's NOT:** Not just "any outdoor event." Implies daytime, openness, playfulness. Not "relaxing" (too passive).

**Why this tag is musically specific:**
Pool Party tracks are usually straightforward and bright. They work in a context where people are primarily social, not focused on the music. Different from Festival Brunch (which is more groovy) or Sunrise Reset (which is transcendent).

---

### Festival Brunch
**When:** Daytime festival context, elevated but social  
**Performance moment:** People are already in a festival headspace, energized but sophisticated  
**Feel:** Groovy, sophisticated but accessible. Fun without trying too hard  
**Real scenario:** 11 am–2 pm, 200+ people at a festival, everyone's already tuned in  
**What it's NOT:** Not "chill"—Festival Brunch has groove and energy. Not as casual as Pool Party.

**Why this tag is musically specific:**
Festival Brunch sits between casual (Pool Party) and deep (Post-game). It respects the listener's engagement while remaining accessible and fun.

---

### Post-game
**When:** After a main event or peak moment; things are winding down but people aren't leaving  
**Performance moment:** The main set is over, energy is descending, people linger  
**Feel:** Reflective, social, maybe melancholic or euphoric. Presence without intensity  
**Real scenario:** Main act just finished. 50 people lingering, chatting, dancing lazily. 2–4 hours before complete wind-down  
**What it's NOT:** Not "sad" or "chill"—Post-game can be joyful. It's about the *timing* (after peak) and *intention* (lingering, not leaving).

**Why this tag is musically specific:**
Post-game is the "after the main thing" moment. A Post-game track has to honor the fact that the party isn't ending abruptly, but something has concluded. It's a different energy than Sunrise Reset (which is transcendent) or Home Stretch (which is actively closing).

---

### Sunrise Reset
**When:** Long night ending; transition toward new day  
**Performance moment:** Literal sunrise, or the equivalent (people exhausted, light changing, world shifting)  
**Feel:** Often introspective or ethereal. Sometimes uplifting. A different energy than night. Renewal  
**Real scenario:** Burning Man camp after-hours. Last people dancing as sun comes up. Exhausted but transcendent  
**What it's NOT:** Not "chillout music"—Sunrise Reset can be energizing. It's about the *moment* (literal or metaphorical sunrise context).

**Why this tag is musically specific:**
Sunrise is a threshold moment. Tracks work here because they acknowledge the shift in time and energy. Not the same as Post-game (which is lingering) or Home Stretch (which is active closing).

## Dimension 2: Journey Role (Functional Purpose in a Set)

**The question:** What does this track *do* in the arc of a set?

These tags describe the structural role a track plays in moving people and energy.

✓ **These are decided and functionally exclusive.**

### Opener
**Purpose:** Establishes the initial energy and emotional tone  
**In a set:** The first track (or first 1–2 tracks). Also: when moving a set into a new zone  
**What it does:** Makes a statement. Could be bold ("I'm here"), subtle ("Let's listen together"), or playful  
**Musical quality:** Sets expectations. Invites people in  
**What it's NOT:** Not necessarily "low energy." An opener can hit hard. It's about making an *entrance*, not setting an intensity level.

**Why this role is musically specific:**
Openers carry the weight of first impression. They need to signal what's possible. "Rising Groove" might be a Pool Party Opener (fun, accessible, welcoming). "Peak Ambient" would be a poor Pool Party Opener (too dense for arrival). Same track can open different moments.

---

### Bridge
**Purpose:** Transitions between two different zones or energies  
**In a set:** Appears between distinct moments. Holds attention while things shift  
**What it does:** Connects two ideas. Could build energy, drop it, or shift mood. The *connective tissue* that moves people forward  
**Musical quality:** Has momentum. Feels inevitable (even though it's transitional)  
**What it's NOT:** Not filler. A bridge is intentional and *earns* its place by serving the transition.

**Why this role is musically specific:**
Bridges are the most versatile role. A Bridge track can work in multiple contexts because its job is to *connect*, not to *be a place*. This is why multi-tagging is powerful for Bridge tracks.

---

### Reset
**Purpose:** Recalibrates the crowd or energy. A moment of breath or reflection  
**In a set:** Comes after intensity or confusion. Clears the air. Reorients  
**What it does:** Gives people permission to reset their bodies and minds. Still part of the party, but differently  
**Musical quality:** Feels like a relief. Creates a pivot point  
**What it's NOT:** Not a letdown or loss of energy. A reset is intentional and still holds presence. Not the same as Home Stretch (which is active closing).

**Why this role is musically specific:**
Reset tracks are structurally important but often subtle. They might be a breakbeat moment, a mood shift, or literally getting quieter. But they're *strategically* placed, not accidental.

---

### Home Stretch
**Purpose:** Moves the set toward closure or rest. Active descent, not fade-out  
**In a set:** The last 3–5 tracks. Moves toward the end with intention and presence  
**What it does:** Winds people down or sends them off with a final emotional note  
**Musical quality:** Could be tender, euphoric, or satisfied. But it's *moving* toward something (not just winding down)  
**What it's NOT:** Not sad or apologetic. Home Stretch can be beautiful and strong. It's about *movement toward ending*, not escape from the party.

**Why this role is musically specific:**
Home Stretch is the closing statement. It has to honor the journey people have been on while moving them toward departure. Not the same as Reset (which is recalibration in the middle) or Bridge (which is connection between moments).

## Dimension 3: Crowd State (Real-Time Dynamics)

**The question:** What's the crowd's attention and engagement mode?

These tags describe real-time crowd dynamics, not just energy levels.

✓ **These are decided and contextually exclusive (one crowd state at a time, though a track can work in multiple states).**

### Arrivals
**Who:** People still coming in. Not everyone has arrived. Energy is building but not unified  
**Attention:** Fragmented. People are checking out the space, finding friends, settling in. Not yet all eyes on you  
**What they're doing:** Social, exploratory. Deciding if they're staying, finding their zone, not fully committed yet  
**Vibe needed:** Welcoming but not demanding. Engaging but not intense. "It's okay to arrive late"  
**Musical quality:** Accessible. Creates FOMO (people want to join) without making latecomers feel awkward

**Why this state is musically specific:**
Arrivals-phase people are split-focused. A track for this moment has to work in the *background of conversation* while also being interesting if someone tunes in. Different from Lock-in (which demands attention).

---

### Lock-in
**Who:** Full crowd, fully present. Everyone's together and committed  
**Attention:** Unified and deep. All eyes (metaphorically) on the music. Full emotional engagement  
**What they're doing:** Watching, feeling, moving with intention. No one's scrolling their phone or chatting over the music  
**Vibe needed:** Engaging, cohesive, building. You can take risks because they're with you  
**Musical quality:** High stakes. Every choice can land or fall flat. But the payoff is real connection.

**Why this state is musically specific:**
Lock-in moments are where the magic happens. Tracks here can be more challenging, more specific, more emotionally direct. The crowd is *listening*, not just present.

---

### Wanderers
**Who:** Crowd is present but distributed. People moving around, chatting, not all facing the booth  
**Attention:** Ambient. They're listening but also socializing. Music is part of the environment, not the center  
**What they're doing:** Conversations, casual dancing, moving between zones. Enjoying but not hyperfocused  
**Vibe needed:** Groovy, permissive, not demanding. Music that's *better* when you're talking over it, not worse  
**Musical quality:** Forgiving. Has pocket and groove that works whether someone's paying attention or not.

**Why this state is musically specific:**
Wanderers moments are about ambient pleasure. A Wanderers-phase track should never feel rejected if someone's not watching. It should *enhance* their experience whether they're focused or not.

## Multi-Dimensional Tagging: How It Works

### Example 1: "Rising Groove"

A track that's building, uplifting, groovy but not overwhelming.

```
Time/Setting:  Pool Party
Journey Role:  Bridge
Crowd State:   Lock-in

Why these tags:
- Pool Party: Daytime, social event. Straightforward energy.
- Bridge: Moves energy forward without jarring. Has momentum.
- Lock-in: The groove is strong enough to hold attention.

In the spatial view:
This track sits at the intersection of Pool Party + Bridge + Lock-in.
It bridges (transitions) within Pool Party while needing a focused crowd.
A DJ browsing might find it via: "Pool Party → Bridge" OR "Lock-in tracks" OR "Bridge → Lock-in transition"
```

---

### Example 2: "Solar Pool" (The Rediscovery Track)

A track that's ethereal, atmospheric, but still groovy. Works in multiple contexts.

```
Time/Setting:  Pool Party, Sunrise Reset
Journey Role:  Bridge
Crowd State:   Lock-in, Wanderers

Why these tags:
- Pool Party + Sunrise Reset: Works in daytime social AND long-night-ending. Unusual but honest.
- Bridge: Transitions between energy states.
- Lock-in + Wanderers: Can work with focused attention OR ambient listening.

In the spatial view:
This track is positioned between multiple zones:
- In Pool Party phase: at the Bridge + Lock-in intersection
- Also visible in Sunrise Reset phase: at the Bridge + Wanderers intersection

It's a "bridge track" across contexts. HIGH REDISCOVERY VALUE.
A DJ browsing finds it multiple ways.
```

---

### Example 3: "Soft Landing"

A track that's tender, closing energy. Meant for people leaving but not sad.

```
Time/Setting:  Post-game, Sunrise Reset
Journey Role:  Home Stretch
Crowd State:   Wanderers

Why these tags:
- Post-game + Sunrise Reset: Both are "winding down" moments.
- Home Stretch: Moves toward closure with beauty.
- Wanderers: Crowd is moving, leaving, but still present.

In the spatial view:
This track sits in the "closure" zones across two contexts.
In Post-game: Home Stretch + Wanderers zone
In Sunrise Reset: Home Stretch + Wanderers zone

Functional purpose is identical; contexts are different.
Same logic, different scenarios.
```

## Tagging Guidelines (For Curators)

### The Three-Question Process

When assigning tags to a new track:

1. **"When would I actually play this?"** → Time/Setting tag(s)
   - Be honest. If it's multiple contexts, tag multiple.
   - If it only works at sunrise, tag only Sunrise Reset (don't force other contexts).

2. **"What does this track *do* in a set? Does it open, bridge, reset, or close?"** → Journey Role tag
   - Most tracks are Bridges. That's fine.
   - Some tracks are pure Openers or pure Home Stretches. Tag accordingly.
   - A track can be a Bridge in one context and a Reset in another (tag both Time tags, one role).

3. **"What kind of crowd attention does this need or work well with?"** → Crowd State tag(s)
   - Lock-in tracks demand presence. Wanderer tracks allow distraction.
   - Some tracks work in both (tag both).
   - Be honest about the track's "forgiveness."

---

### Common Tagging Mistakes to Avoid

**Too vague:**
- ❌ "This is groovy" (Not a tag. Which context? Which role?)
- ✓ "This is a Bridge track for Pool Party with Wanderers crowd"

**Over-specific:**
- ❌ Creating 10 custom sub-categories
- ✓ Using the nine core tags; let spatial proximity add nuance

**Ignoring dimensions:**
- ❌ "This is a Festival Brunch Opener" (Incomplete—what crowd state?)
- ✓ "Festival Brunch Opener for Arrivals energy"

**Forcing multi-tags unnecessarily:**
- ❌ Every track tagged with 2–3 time settings (probably not honest)
- ✓ Most tracks belong to one time setting; some genuinely work across two

## Why This Tagging System Is Musically Honest

**It respects musical context, not just abstract categorization.**

A track that works as a Bridge in Pool Party might be a Reset in Sunrise Reset. Same track, different *role*, different *context*. This isn't reductive; it's *precise*.

The system acknowledges that music is contextual. A DJ's intelligence isn't in sorting by BPM. It's in understanding: "This track works here, for this reason, with this crowd."

That's what this system represents.
