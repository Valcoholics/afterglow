## The Three Pitches (For Different Contexts)

### 30-Second Hook (Elevator pitch)
"DJ gets texted: can you play a pool party in 2 hours? Current tools make them browse 500 tracks by metadata. We let them see their Pool Party tracks in one organized view—organized by *how they actually think*: who's here, what role does this track play, when would I play it? Faster prep, rediscovery, confidence."

### 60-Second Story (Full problem→solution)
"**Problem:** DJs organize libraries by metadata (genre, BPM) because that's what tools support. But when prepping a gig, they navigate by context: when would I play this? What crowd state? What role does it play? Especially for emerging DJs in spontaneous scenarios, that mismatch is painful.

**Insight:** If we organize by *DJ decision-making dimensions* instead of metadata, library browsing becomes discovery instead of searching.

**Solution:** Three-layer model: Timeline (choose your context) → Constellation (spatial map of your library organized by performance contexts) → Track details (why this track belongs here).

**Benefit:** DJ preps a pool party in 10 minutes instead of 30+, rediscovers forgotten material, feels confident."

### 5–7 Minute Walkthrough (Full demo)
1. Open Afterglow → Timeline shows 5 day phases (30 sec)
2. Click Pool Party → Constellation loads (Arrivals, Lock-in, Wanderers zones) (90 sec)
3. Explain spatial logic: X-axis = Journey Role, Y-axis = Crowd State (60 sec)
4. Hover to show proximity relationships (30 sec)
5. Click on a track, see details → click on the hero underplayed track (60 sec)
6. The arc: "This track is here because it's a Bridge for Lock-in crowd. I haven't played it in 6 months but it's perfect." (45 sec)
7. Close: "That's Afterglow. Organize your library by how you actually DJ." (30 sec)

## What Judges Will See

### Richie (Artistry Focus)
He's looking for: Is DJ agency central, or does the system presume to recommend?

**What he'll see:** You picked these tags, you organized these tracks, the system serves *your* judgment. No automation. No algorithm saying "you should play this." Just your library, organized your way.

### Carlos (Musical Sophistication)
He's looking for: Does this reduce music to mood tags, or reflect actual DJ thinking?

**What he'll see:** Tags are "Bridge" + "Lock-in" + "Pool Party," not "feels chill" or "vibes happy." The spatial arrangement shows musical relationships (proximity = DJ adjacency). That's sophisticated thinking.

### Yan (Design Intentionality)
He's looking for: Is every visual choice justified, or is it decoration?

**What he'll see:** Spatial positions aren't algorithmic; they're hardcoded based on your understanding of the music. Hover interaction proves spacing is functional (nearby tracks respond). No mystery, no "why is this here?"

### Romain (Product-Market Fit)
He's looking for: Is this solving a real DJ problem? Is the scope realistic?

**What he'll see:** Pool Party scenario is specific and real. Tracks are authentic (not dummy data). MVP is one phase, not five half-finished ones. Scope is honest.

### Michele (Human-Centered Design)
She's looking for: Can someone walk in and understand this in 30 seconds?

**What he'll see:** Zone labels are clear (Arrivals, Lock-in, Wanderers). Interaction is intuitive (hover shows relationships). Rediscovery moment is meaningful (not a surprise, but a "yes, I should have remembered").

### JB (Product Thinking)
He's looking for: Does this team understand MVP thinking? Or are they over-promising?

**What he'll see:** One Pool Party phase (not five). Explicit roadmap (these are the other phases, future). Honest about dependencies (all decisions made, blockers identified).

## Pre-Demo Confidence Checklist

**Can you answer these without hesitation?**

1. **"Why Pool Party only?"** → "One beautiful, fully realized phase proves the concept works. Three half-finished phases would be scattered. Scope is intentional."

2. **"Why not algorithmic positioning?"** → "Because DJ thinking is sophisticated and personal. Algorithmic would be generic. Hardcoded lets me defend every choice. You can trace why each track is where it is."

3. **"Why these three dimensions?"** → "Because every DJ answers these three questions when prepping: When? Why? Who? Time/Setting, Journey Role, Crowd State. Not reductive; comprehensive."

4. **"Why real tracks, not dummy data?"** → "Because authenticity matters. Dummy data looks clean but doesn't prove the concept works with real libraries. Real tracks show real DJ thinking."

5. **"Why is the rediscovery moment important?"** → "Because it proves the system does what we built it for: helps DJs prep faster and rediscover forgotten material. It's the emotional payoff."

6. **"What happens after the hackathon?"** → "Same approach for other contexts (Sunrise Reset, Festival Brunch, etc.), djay integration, user accounts, algorithmic tag suggestions, analytics. But MVP is Pool Party only."

## If a Judge Asks a Tough Question

**General approach:** Be authentic. They respect thoughtful designers who can explain decisions.

- **If they challenge the scope:** "Intentionally narrow. One polished phase > three scattered. We're proving the concept, not trying to do everything."

- **If they ask about automation:** "DJ decides everything. System organizes. We respect musical sophistication; automation would be presumptuous."

- **If they ask about real-world integration:** "djay integration is the natural next step. This is the prep layer; djay is the performance layer."

- **If they ask about scale:** "Tested with 150 real tracks, 40–50 per phase. Concept scales. Libraries of thousands would work."

Stay calm. Land the rediscovery moment. That's the most important beat. Judges will forgive timing if the concept lands.
